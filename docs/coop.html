Co-op

Up to 4 wizards in one run, invited with a link.

Starting a party

From the main menu, Multiplayer → Host party opens a lobby with four seats and Copy invite link. Paste the link to your friends: clicking it opens the game and drops them into your lobby, or they can copy it and press Join from link on the same screen, which reads it off the clipboard. Each player picks their own starting family on their seat with the arrows; the host picks the difficulty and presses Start run. Everyone needs the same version of the game: a different one is turned away at the door.

Reaching the host

The host's game listens on UDP port 27420 and asks the router to open it (UPnP); the lobby says whether that worked. When it does not, players on the same network can still join, and friends elsewhere need the host to forward UDP 27420 to their PC by hand. A link carries both the host's public address and its address on the local network, and the game tries both.

Who is who

Each seat has a colour, worn on the wizard's robe, its orb, the ring at its feet, its name over its head and its bar in the party list at the bottom left. An arrow at the edge of the screen points at a teammate, or a shrine, that is out of sight.

Harder with more

Each wizard past the first adds 50% more enemies to a wave, and to how many can be alive at once, and 35% more health to every enemy, bosses included. Enemy damage is unchanged. Abilities and cards that deal flat damage still scale with the wave alone, so a bigger party is a bigger fight rather than a weaker one.

Your run, together

Everyone plays their own build in a shared world. Each wizard has their own cards, essence, Essence Shop, rerolls and banishes, ability, companions, combo and score. When a wave ends, every wizard is dealt their own hand, and the world waits until everyone has picked (the screen says who it is waiting for). An enemy chases whichever wizard is nearest, and keeps chasing it unless another comes clearly closer. A kill, and everything a kill pays out (essence, kill-triggered cards, score), goes to whoever landed it, including burns and other damage over time. Drops go to whoever reaches them.

Falling, and the shrines

A wizard who goes down leaves a ghost where they fell, and a shrine rises 7–11 m away, with a ring 2.2 m across. The rest of the party revives them by doing the shrine's task: an Offering counts enemies killed inside the ring, by anyone and by anything; a Vigil counts seconds spent standing in it, and drains back at 0.5 s a second while nobody is there, so it has to be held rather than visited. A boss wave has no crowd to offer, so its shrines are always a Vigil. Done, the wizard stands up in the ring at 50% health with 2 s in which nothing can touch them. A downed player watches whoever is nearest, still picks cards between waves, and sees how far along their shrine is. The run ends only when the whole party is down at once.

What changes

No hitstop: one wizard's hit freezing the world would stall the other three. The pause menu and [U] do not stop the world, since it is not yours alone, and the F3 cheat menu is off. Co-op runs are not recorded in your personal bests; achievements count what you did, not what a teammate did.

How it stays in step

Every machine in the party runs the whole game itself from the same seed, and the only thing sent is each player's controls, a few ticks ahead of when they are used. The delay is set from the party's ping when the run starts, and the run moves at the pace of its slowest connection: if someone's input is late, everyone waits for it (the screen names who). Each machine fingerprints its world every two seconds and the host compares them, so if the copies ever disagree the game says out of sync rather than playing on quietly wrong. A player who leaves mid-run is gone from that moment; the rest play on.
